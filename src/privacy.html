VI Translator

Privacy policy

Last updated: {{updated}}

VI Translator is a free, open-source app that reads a game's subtitles and shows them translated. This page covers the desktop app (Windows, macOS, Linux), the TV apps (Android TV, LG webOS, Samsung Tizen) and this website. There are no accounts, no ads and no trackers.

In short

  • The game picture and screenshots never leave your computer, except a Snapshot's picture when the service you chose for it reads pictures.
  • The desktop app sends us nothing about you or what you play. Since version 1.3.1 it sends no game lines: it only downloads translations from us and new versions from GitHub.
  • The TV apps only talk to your own computer, on your home network.
  • We don't sell or share data, and we don't use it for advertising.

Desktop app

Game lines (no longer sent)

Since version 1.3.1, the app sends no game lines to our server, and nothing else about you or what you play. The text it reads on screen stays on your computer, except what goes to the translation service you choose (below).

Earlier versions had a Help improve translations feature that sent the English lines read on screen (subtitles, instructions, captions) to vitranslator.app. That feature is gone. The lines collected before are kept on the server to build translations, stored only with a one-way hash of a random install ID: no readable install ID and no names.

Translation services you choose

The app translates on your computer by default. The first time, it downloads the translation model from Hugging Face (huggingface.co); after that it works offline. If you choose an online service instead (such as DeepL, Google, Microsoft Azure, LibreTranslate, Claude or ChatGPT) and enter your own key for it, the lines to translate go straight from your computer to that service, under its own privacy policy. If the service you choose for Snapshot reads pictures, the Snapshot's picture goes to it the same way. Your keys are stored encrypted on your computer and are never sent to us.

Updates and game data

The app checks GitHub about once an hour for a new version, and downloads game information and translation data from vitranslator.app. These requests carry no personal data; like any web request, they reveal your IP address to the server that answers.

Phone and OBS pages

If you turn on the phone page or the OBS overlay, the app serves the translations on your home network only, to devices that open the address it shows. Nothing goes over the internet.

TV apps

The Android TV, LG webOS and Samsung Tizen apps show the translations your computer sends them over your home network. To pair, the TV shows a code that you type in the desktop app; the TV then remembers that computer's address and a pairing token. The TV apps send nothing to us or to anyone else, and collect nothing about what you watch. The input-source setting (show on one HDMI input) is read from the TV and stays on the TV. Disconnect on the TV, or removing the TV in the desktop app, erases the pairing.

This website

vitranslator.app sets no cookies and runs no analytics. It is served through Cloudflare, which processes requests (including IP addresses) to deliver and protect the site, under Cloudflare's privacy policy.

What the server keeps, and for how long

  • Game lines collected by Help improve translations before version 1.3.1: kept to build and review translations. They are game text, not personal data. No new lines are collected.
  • Install ID hashes: kept with those lines. No readable install IDs and no names are stored.
  • IP addresses: used in memory only, to limit how many requests one address can make per hour; never stored in the database. The web server's standard access logs, which include IP addresses, are deleted after 14 days.

Children

The apps aren't aimed at children and don't knowingly collect any personal information from anyone.

Your choices

  • Everything the apps store stays on your devices: the TV apps' data goes when you uninstall them, and the desktop app's data is in its folders (Documents\VI Translator, Pictures\VI Translator and the app's settings folder), which you can delete.
  • Because the server stores no name, email or readable install ID, it can't tell which lines are yours. For any question or request, open an issue on GitHub.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date.
